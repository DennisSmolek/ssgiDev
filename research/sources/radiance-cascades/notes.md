## Primer: what radiance cascades actually is (10-15 sentences)

Radiance cascades is a global-illumination data structure, introduced by Alexander Sannikov (Grinding Gear Games) for Path of Exile 2 and first shown publicly at ExileCon 2023, that stores and reconstructs a scene's radiance field without per-pixel stochastic ray tracing or temporal denoising ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/radiance-cascades-new-approach-to-calculating-global-illumination), [sannikov-paper-notes.md](sannikov-paper-notes.md)). Its foundational insight is the **penumbra hypothesis**: resolving light correctly at a given distance from its source requires higher *spatial* resolution and lower *angular* resolution nearby, and the inverse — lower spatial, higher angular — far away; this relationship is provably necessary and sufficient for a discretization of the radiance field to remain accurate at every scale ([sannikov-paper-notes.md](sannikov-paper-notes.md)). The technique operationalizes this by organizing storage into a **hierarchy of cascades**: cascade 0 has many densely-packed probes each casting only a few rays (high spatial, low angular resolution), and each successive cascade halves probe density while doubling angular sample count per probe, keeping total ray count roughly constant per level in the "flatland"/volumetric case ([sannikov-paper-notes.md](sannikov-paper-notes.md); worked numeric example in [gmshaders-radiance-cascades-part1.md](gmshaders-radiance-cascades-part1.md): 256 probes × 64 rays = 4 probes × 4,096 rays = 16,384 total rays either way). Each probe doesn't store a full ray — it stores a **radiance interval**, `L_{a,b}(p, ω̂)`, the incoming radiance along direction ω̂ bounded between distances `a` and `b` from the probe, rather than the unbounded radiance a normal ray-trace would compute ([sannikov-paper-notes.md](sannikov-paper-notes.md)). Successive cascades cover successively farther, wider intervals — e.g. cascade *i*'s interval starts around `2^i` times cascade 0's — so together the cascades tile the full distance range without any single cascade needing to trace arbitrarily far. The critical operation that reconstructs a usable radiance field from this hierarchy is **merging**: adjacent intervals combine via `L_{a,c} = L_{a,b} + β·L_{b,c}` (transmittance-weighted addition), and cross-cascade merging (cascade N+1 into cascade N) uses bilinear interpolation across each probe's 4 spatially-nearest neighbors in the coarser cascade, gated by a visibility term that suppresses blending across occlusion boundaries ([m4xc-fundamentals-of-rc.md](m4xc-fundamentals-of-rc.md), [gmshaders-radiance-cascades-part1.md](gmshaders-radiance-cascades-part1.md)). This merge-based reconstruction is what lets the method claim "infinitely many rays in finite time" — each cascade level only costs about half as much as the previous while doubling the effective ray count, so the geometric series of costs converges to roughly 2× the cost of cascade 0 alone, regardless of how many cascades (i.e. how far) you extend ([sannikov-paper-notes.md](sannikov-paper-notes.md)). Crucially, the *memory* scaling law differs by domain: in flatland (2D+1D) and true 3D volumetric space (3D+2D), per-cascade memory shrinks geometrically so total memory across all cascades converges to a small constant multiple of cascade 0; but for **surface radiance** — a 2D surface (like a screen or G-buffer) carrying 2D directional data — per-cascade memory stays *constant* rather than shrinking, so total memory grows *linearly* with cascade count ([sannikov-paper-notes.md](sannikov-paper-notes.md), §Analysis). That surface-radiance case is exactly the situation a screen-space GI implementation lives in, which is why RC's celebrated "constant cost" framing needs qualification for screen-space use. The technique is otherwise notable for being **geometry-agnostic and constant-cost**: demoed at ~12ms across scenes with 2, 102, and 1,002 light-emitting particles, and as low as 0.3ms/frame on a GTX 970 for the base 2D case with no denoising ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/radiance-cascades-new-approach-to-calculating-global-illumination)).

## Maturity assessment

- **2D flatland RC is genuinely settled.** Community consensus (radiance.wiki, the paper, multiple independent from-scratch reimplementations) treats 2D as solved and "noiseless" — dozens of implementations exist across GameMaker, GLSL/Shadertoy, Vulkan/Slang, Godot, and Unity, and they converge on the same probe/interval/merge recipe with only performance-layer variation (pre-averaging, direction-first layout). See [implementations-survey.md](implementations-survey.md), [gmshaders-radiance-cascades-part2.md](gmshaders-radiance-cascades-part2.md).
- **What actually shipped in PoE2 is a screen-space-only variant, not full 3D RC.** The paper is explicit: "PoE2's screenspace implementation cannot capture offscreen lighting or occluders" ([sannikov-paper-notes.md](sannikov-paper-notes.md)). PoE2's probes scale as `~2^i × 1` (screen-tied), not `~2^i × 2^i` (true volumetric) — it's cheap specifically because it gave up offscreen light capture.
- **True 3D world-space RC is unsolved / impractical at the naive level.** The paper itself says storing a 3D grid for cascade 0 alone is "practically equivalent to voxelizing the entire scene." tmpvar's own dense-grid 3D repo is non-functional/abandoned-in-place ([implementations-survey.md](implementations-survey.md)). radiance.wiki calls 3D explicitly "an open 3D challenge... active research area."
- **The screen-space-probe / world-space-interval hybrid (SPWI, §4.5 of the paper) is the active frontier and the one most relevant to this project.** It has: a named section in the original paper, a dedicated academic thesis (Kyriakos Gavras, Chalmers), and at least one working three.js implementation (three-rc) that live-toggles between world-space-BVH and pure-screen-space tracing. It is real and usable today, but not yet a single converged best practice — different implementers make different calls on ray-tracing vs ray-marching, and on upscaling method. See [screen-space-radiance-cascades.md](screen-space-radiance-cascades.md).
- **Academic follow-up is thin but exists and includes Sannikov himself**: the 2025 "Holographic Radiance Cascades" arXiv paper co-authored by Sannikov is 2D-only despite the name, and pushes performance further (1.85ms @ 512², 7.67ms @ 1024² on an RTX 3080 Laptop). No peer-reviewed 3D/screen-space paper was found — the closest is a Bachelor's/Master's-level thesis (Chalmers) and an honours project (Noam Zeise), i.e. this remains largely industry/community-driven rather than academically formalized.
- **A real, unresolved caveat surfaced repeatedly**: the celebrated "constant cost independent of scene complexity" claim is specifically a *volumetric/flatland* scaling result; for the surface-radiance (screen-space) case memory scales linearly with cascade count, and offscreen light is simply invisible unless you pay for the more expensive world-space interval tracing. Any SSGI design built on RC needs to budget for that explicitly rather than assume the flatland scaling story carries over unmodified.

## How RC might complement or replace a VBAO-style near-field pass

No source found in this sweep directly proposes an RC+VBAO hybrid — this appears to be genuinely unexplored territory in the literature gathered, so the following is this research's own synthesis, not a cited claim:

- Cascade 0 is RC's most expensive, most spatially-demanding level ("calculating just cascade 0 in a high level of detail can take milliseconds, which can already be over budget" — [sannikov-paper-notes.md](sannikov-paper-notes.md)), yet it's also exactly the regime a fast horizon/visibility-bitmask AO pass is already good at: very-near-field occlusion and bent-normal estimation at full screen resolution.
- A plausible architecture: let a VBAO-style pass supply the near-field visibility/AO term at full resolution (replacing or heavily discounting cascade 0's need for full spatial density), and let RC cascades 1+ handle the mid/far-field diffuse bounce where lower spatial resolution is already tolerable by RC's own design. This would sidestep RC's most expensive tier rather than trying to make it cheaper.
- The one source that stages RC directly next to SSAO visually (Noam Zeise's dissertation, [academic-followups.md](academic-followups.md)) is worth a full manual read before committing to this architecture — this sweep only captured a compressed summary of it, not the full comparison.
- Practically, the three-rc repo's dual-mode tracing (world-space BVH vs. screen-space raymarch, [three-rc-implementation.md](three-rc-implementation.md)) demonstrates that mixing tracing strategies by distance/cascade-level inside one RC pipeline is already an established pattern — extending that pattern to "AO pass for near field, RC for far field" is a small conceptual step from what's already shipped, not a novel architecture.

## Best 3 sources for a future implementer (in recommended reading order)

1. **tmpvar's interactive explainer** — https://tmpvar.com/poc/radiance-cascades/ — build intuition hands-on before reading any code; no other source lets you literally drag sliders and watch cascades/probes/intervals update live.
2. **jason.today/rc** — https://jason.today/rc — the most implementation-literal walkthrough found (WebGL, real code, explicit gotchas like sRGB handling and edge-clamping during merge) — closest in spirit to "I want to write this myself, in my own renderer, today."
3. **CodyJasonBennett/three-rc** — https://github.com/CodyJasonBennett/three-rc — the only source that is simultaneously (a) three.js, (b) genuinely 3D, (c) screen-space-probe-based with a live toggle between world-space-BVH and screen-space tracing, i.e. a working demonstration of exactly the SPWI hybrid this project's SSGI stack would want to build toward.

(Runner-up: the paper itself, `sannikov-paper.pdf` / `sannikov-paper-notes.md`, is non-negotiable primary-source reading once the above three have built intuition — it's just not the easiest on-ramp.)
